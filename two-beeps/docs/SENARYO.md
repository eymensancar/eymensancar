# Two Beeps — seslendirme metni (10 bölüm)

Okuma: sakin, hikâye anlatır gibi. Her bölüm ayrı ses dosyası. Tırnak içindeki cümleler başka birinin sözü; sesini biraz değiştirmen yeter.

---

## Part 1 · The Chimney

Grandpa left me three things. A crane. A debt. And a list with one line I'm not allowed to read.

Ten buildings to take down. Nine in old black ink. The tenth is scratched out. In blue. And the blue is fresh.

The crane is called Daisy. She is older than my dad, and she hates mornings.

Job one: an old chimney in an empty lot. The night before, I flattened forty towers in Demolition Master. In the game, you just try again.

Real chimneys don't.

I pulled the lever. Daisy beeped. Twice. I ignored her.

The chimney fell the wrong way. Nobody was near. My professor says that's called luck.

In the bricks I found a stamp. Two letters. H and K.

And I keep thinking about that beep. Daisy beeped twice before it fell. Like she knew.

---

## Part 2 · The Ant

My sister calls me Ant. Only one other person ever did. Grandpa.

Defne is an engineer. A real one. She came to the yard with coffee and bad news.

"Sell it, Ant. The crane, the land, the list. You are nineteen."

I showed her the stamp from the chimney. H and K. She went quiet. Then she changed the subject.

We tried Grandpa's office. The door was locked. And when I drove Daisy past it, she beeped. Twice.

There was nothing on the hook. Nothing.

Defne looked at my phone. "Study notes?" It was not study notes. It was a tower, halfway down. I put it in my pocket.

Then I checked the mailbox. One letter. From the city.

Someone had reported me. Unsafe work.

It was signed: Ironline Demolition. Nobody in this town has ever heard of them.

---

## Part 3 · Mr. Hale

A man in a white car offered me more money than this whole yard is worth. For a piece of paper.

His name is Mr. Hale. He builds half the city. He wants Grandpa's list.

"Not the crane. Not the land. Just the list."

I said I would think about it. He counted the money anyway. I thought: in Demolition Master, gold buys upgrades. Here it buys Daisy one used tire.

Job two was a small water tower. This time, a licensed crew, a fence, everyone far back. It came down clean.

Same stamp in the concrete. H and K.

Mr. Hale saw it. He stopped in the middle of a sentence. Then he drove away.

That evening, a second letter came. Ironline again. One line.

"The ant is not ready."

Whoever is writing these knows what Grandpa called me.

---

## Part 4 · Two Beeps

Grandpa's phone had one app open. I did not expect it to be mine.

I finally got into his office. Dust, drawings, cold tea. And his phone, still on the charger.

On the screen: Demolition Master. Level twelve. He was stuck. Grandpa. Who took down real buildings for forty years.

I finished the level for him. It took me three tries.

Then I looked at the drawings. They were of Daisy. Grandpa built something into her. A sensor. When the ground or the load is wrong, she beeps. Twice. It means: stop.

So Daisy was never grumpy. Daisy was warning me. At the chimney, she was right.

Then I saw the calendar on the wall. It was hanging very low. I moved it.

Behind it, a crack. From the floor to the ceiling.

And I remembered. Daisy beeped at this door.

---

## Part 5 · H and K

H is for Hale. K is for Kaya. Kaya is my last name.

I found it in Grandpa's notebook. Forty years ago, he and Mr. Hale were partners. They built fast. They built cheap. Nine buildings.

The same nine on my list.

On the last page, Grandpa wrote: "They will not last. I should have said no. Take them down before they fall."

So the list is not a job. It is an apology.

Defne read it over my shoulder. She was quiet for a long time. Then she asked, "How are your exams going?"

I said, "Look, a bird." There was no bird.

I opened the game on my phone, just to not look at her.

Then I read the ninth address again. Slowly.

Number nine is not old. It has forty floors. And people still work in it.

---

## Part 6 · Ironline

I found Ironline Demolition. It was sitting across from me, eating my fries.

The third letter came on a Tuesday. Same name. But this time I looked at the envelope. There was a coffee ring on it. And only two people ever called me Ant.

I put it on the table. Defne did not even pretend.

"You were nineteen and alone on a crane, Ant. I wanted them to stop you. Before a building did."

I wanted to be angry. It started raining instead. We sat in Daisy's cabin and shared one phone, like when we were kids. Demolition Master, one turn each. She did Grandpa's voice. "Plan first. Then press the button."

"I'll help you finish the list," she said. "As your engineer."

Then she got serious.

"But first, tell me the truth. Who is this professor you keep quoting?"

---

## Part 7 · The Professor

I have been quoting my professor since the first video. I don't have a professor.

I left engineering school in March. I sat down for an exam. I knew the answers. My hands just stopped.

I walked out and never went back. I told nobody. That night I played Demolition Master until morning.

It did not teach me engineering. A game can't do that. It just reminded me that I like working out where to start.

Defne did not shout. She said, "Okay. Then stop lying to Daisy too." And she moved her desk into the yard.

With her signature, the city gave us our permit back. Three, four, five. Three more old buildings came down, slow and safe.

Then she opened Grandpa's reports on number nine. She read for an hour. Then she went pale.

"Ant. Number nine is already falling. Just very slowly."

---

## Part 8 · Number Nine

Building number nine has Mr. Hale's name on the door. In gold.

Six, seven and eight came down in the autumn. That left the big one. Hale Tower. Grandpa's reports say the foundation is cracking. A little more every year.

We took the reports to the city. They told us to take a number and wait. Mr. Hale was already there. Waiting too.

For an hour nobody spoke. Then I saw his phone. He was playing the same game as me. Badly.

He looked at my screen. "You rush the first move. So did Kaya."

Then he said the rest, quietly.

"The cheap concrete was my idea. When it went wrong, your grandfather signed the papers. He took the blame. I took the company. I wanted your list so nobody would ever read it."

They called his number. He stood up.

"He carried it for forty years. I can carry the last one. Empty my tower."

---

## Part 9 · The Empty Tower

Two hundred people walked out of a tower today. Almost nobody knew the whole reason.

It took six weeks. Not us. Specialists, from three cities. Our job was small: clear the street, and wait behind the line.

The night before, I did not open the game. I did not need practice. I needed sleep.

At noon, forty floors sat down into their own dust. Slowly. Exactly where the engineers wanted. Nobody got a scratch.

Mr. Hale watched from beside Daisy. He took off his hat. Daisy stayed silent. For once.

Nine buildings. Nine lines in black ink. Done.

That night I held the list up to the lamp. The blue ink was fresh. Grandpa scratched that line out this year. With his own pen.

Under it, I could finally read an address.

I knew that address. I have known it my whole life.

---

## Part 10 · Build Something

The last building on Grandpa's list is the one I grew up in.

Line ten is our yard. His office. The cracked wall behind the calendar. Daisy tried to tell me on day two.

He wrote it down. Then he scratched it out. I think he could not ask me.

So I did not wait to be asked.

We emptied the office. Defne signed the plan. Mr. Hale carried boxes. And Daisy made her last lift.

Under the first stone there was a tin. Inside, a note. Grandpa's handwriting.

"Ant. Now build something."

I go back to school on Monday. Defne is drawing a new office. Small. Strong.

Daisy is retired. She sits in the yard, in the sun.

This morning she beeped. Twice. I checked everything.

Then I understood. She was just saying goodbye.
